# How the chess arm became controllable from one terminal

**Bench result, 20–21 November 2026.** The replacement-board follower completed recorded, guarded moves to two g5 handoffs and an empty e4 hold. One g5 startup actually produced the troublesome mixed torque sample and then completed successfully. This is the evidence for reliable operation on this setup. An individual bus read can still fail, a path can still trip a guard, and the physical arm can still fall when power is removed.

Use the [terminal operator guide](ARM_OPERATOR_GUIDE.md) to run the arm and the [Codex retry and ownership guide](CODEX_ARM_HANDOFF.md) during a live incident. This history explains why those procedures exist. Archived experiment scripts and gain values are evidence from particular runs, not commissioning instructions for another arm.

## What went wrong and what changed

| Incident | What the evidence showed | How we operate now |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical concern and board exchange | The follower kept its six servos and original 12 V supply. The replacement USB board had the same printed reference but a different USB serial, changing from 5B7B016162 to 5B7B014135. Supply voltage was not recorded during the failed post-swap seed. The swap alone cannot explain a given timeout. | Select the stable device-by-ID path and robot ID in the operator guide. Match the calibration against the motors and check live registers. Later powered travel established that the arm was usable. [Service note](../runs/robot-calibration/powered-placement-20261120/board-replacement-20261120-171724/service-note.txt) |
| USB device present but six motors missing | After the access-permission check the adapter opened, yet discovery found zero of six expected model-777 servos. A cable retry also recorded a port connection failure. These are different failure stages. A servo LED does not verify USB access, bus communication or all six torque states. | Inspect device identity, permission and ownership first. With exclusive access, use a passive read and require every expected ID. Later the saved calibration matched all six; a 3° wrist-roll test produced 2.73° travel and returned within 0.35° of its start. This proved movement on that axis. [Board and wrist-response records](../runs/robot-calibration/powered-placement-20261120/board-replacement-20261120-171724/) |
| A new chat inherited history but lacked the old terminal | A previous controller could still own the port in powered recovery. Starting a second reader would compete for replies. The prompt disappearing from a chat says nothing about motor power. | Identify PID, TTY, port owner, run directory and fresh recovery events without opening the bus. Continue on the owning terminal. If it needs a human handoff, identify the terminal and exact pending action once. |
| Gains differed between runs | One paired run stopped on unexpected gains. A passive restoration record shows all P gains at 16 after that particular intervention. The later successful g5 run recorded P values 16/64/64/32/16/16. Assuming either profile universally would misidentify real settings. Separate lock operations also returned a checksum error or timed out. | Open the bus through the pinned adapter, read actual P/I/D and torque limits for each motor, and match the reviewed plan. Verify lock writes by addressed readback. Investigate the failing transaction before any new write. [Gain record](../runs/robot-calibration/powered-placement-20261120/board-replacement-20261120-171724/cable-connected-01/gain-restoration.json) |
| A position write unexpectedly energized the arm | A single broadcast of current positions produced torque-on flags without an explicit enable command. One early sample reported 1,0,0,0,0,0 about 1.16 ms after the pre-write timestamp. Recovery saw all six on by 18.99 ms. Another run first reported zeroes and saw all on by 25.77 ms. Host timestamps cannot identify exactly when each drive engaged. | Treat the seed as an energizing write. Prepare supervised recovery first, verify the stationary start, seed once, then verify by reading. The two-pose wrist-pitch experiment stopped before motion and was retired. No firmware explanation or vendor intervention was needed to demonstrate the later guarded workflow. [Packet observations](../runs/robot-calibration/powered-placement-20261120/offline-startup-review-01/feetech-startup-question.txt) |
| One early mixed sample aborted otherwise plausible startups | Repeated e4 and d4 attempts stopped immediately after seeding. Later all-on recovery samples did not retroactively authorize the cancelled trajectory. | During startup the owner polls within the original 0.5-second deadline. Require two consecutive complete all-on verifications with goal, fault, lock, drift and temperature checks. A mixed sample keeps the system in verification. A torque drop during movement cancels the path. |
| Reads timed out or returned a checksum error | Examples include a group goal read timing out on ID 1, an addressed goal read timing out on ID 2, a torque checksum error on ID 5 and a torque timeout on ID 6. An error naming one motor in a group request does not report torque off on that motor. | Read seed goals by motor address. During seed verification permit one discarded torque-checksum or addressed goal-timeout read, with additional state checks. Never merge partial packets or replay the seed. Before any write, an independent passive capture can be repeated after the bus settles and the previous reader exits. A fresh power cycle is not the default response. |
| Reconnecting changed the starting pose | The arm can settle under load or move when supported. f4 plans based on earlier ticks failed their start check. Repeated restarts also cost time and made it harder to compare the same pose. Startup delay was a plausible contributor to some timeouts; it was not established as their common cause. | Capture the actual start, replan and check the path. On a verified powered hold, take a snapshot and plan the next target from files while the original controller remains connected. Compare the last goal and live feedback at continuation. |
| Long moves failed tracking or travel checks | d6 exceeded active-joint tracking; early holds exceeded an elbow threshold; a paired move exceeded even 26 ticks. Other runs reached a pose and later crossed the actual-position envelope. “Reached,” “held throughout” and “stopped before motion” have different evidence. | The host streams small goals on a 0.1 s schedule. The servos perform local position control. At each checkpoint verify the previous goal, actual ticks, allowed travel and timing before continuing. Intermediate elbow tolerance differs from arrival tolerance. Keep the guard even when the area around the piece is empty; lateral clearance does not establish tracking or table clearance. |
| Temperature, timing and input can interrupt a run | A high or invalid sensor value, a stalled video stream, a late feedback batch or a lost terminal prevents verified operation. These are guarded conditions; the archive below identifies which actually produced recorded errors. | Keep timing and camera checks. The current temperature guard immediately stops at 45 °C on arm joints or 60 °C on the gripper. A gripper reading from 45 to below 60 °C permits three addressed checks without a goal write; each must be below 45 °C within the confirmation window. See [implemented guards](collection/placement_guards.py). |
| Ending the program could be mistaken for releasing torque | The arm falls when torque goes off. A recovery flag can report operator-confirmed removal without subsequent telemetry. A supported ruler reading also differs mechanically from a free powered hold. | Stop the path, keep supervised recovery, support the arm and piece, and let the operator physically remove power and confirm it. Never automate release. Record support state. Between healthy holds keep the same controller and supply. |

## Placement problems that continued after communication worked

- **Goal, encoder and fingertip heights differed.** At g4, an 80 mm request produced about 77.1 mm from measured encoders and 69.2 ± 1 mm on the ruler. Better shaft tracking alone cannot remove the second discrepancy. A local 7.75 mm model-high correction was fitted on e4/f4. A subsequent 70 mm physical request at g4 measured 68.7 ± 1 mm. The 5 mm clearance remains unvalidated. [Ruler data, uncertainties and source analysis](../runs/robot-calibration/powered-placement-20261120/powered-placement-results.md)
- **Measurement references changed.** Corrected ruler values at one pose count once. Record the datum and 3.7 mm conversion where applicable. Hover parallax limits horizontal measurements; a visual centring report has no measured millimetre uncertainty. A late reading during recovery may have uncertain timing or support load.
- **Board, piece and clearance assumptions changed.** Marker 22 was loose and later resecured; affected absolute registrations carry extra uncertainty. The board and knight also moved between setups. Use the current piece location, distinguish its base diameter at the board from the widest protruding ring, and compare the actual open-jaw gap at the relevant height. Apply the fingertip height bias only to its validated local use. Evaluate shoulder and other link clearance separately. [Higher g5 clearance evidence](../runs/robot-calibration/wrist-mount-validation-20261120/g5-higher-handoff-01/)
- **Synthetic views initially disagreed with the real arm.** The original wrist render put the fingertips at the top of the image. Screw-constrained mounting, corrected image-up, measured lens and the 1.50× recording crop fixed the gross orientation. The remaining 14–16 px pad-edge discrepancy largely came from rendering bare jaws. Adding taped surfaces reduced vertical edge errors to 2–3 px in the three tested holds. This was a visual change; the wrist camera had not been remounted. [Pad replay](../runs/robot-calibration/wrist-mount-validation-20261120/screw-pad-edge-replay/results.json)
- **Accurate-looking pads still left a board error.** Held-out joint offsets improved the first g5 wrist board error from 21.1 to 2.2 px and empty e4 from 16.1 to 1.0 px. Fixed-side g5 still missed by 13.9 px. Combining another registration with joint offsets improved its wrist view but worsened agreement with the fixed camera. These remain offline candidates. Powered control is demonstrated; repeatable low pickups, loaded transfers and universal board accuracy require separate evidence. [Cross-pose comparison](../runs/robot-calibration/wrist-mount-validation-20261120/heldout-board-encoder-render/comparison.json)

## Three successful runs that show the change

Counts below are logged command and feedback events, including repeated ramp or checkpoint commands. Each row is one recording.

| Recording | Commands / feedback batches | Arrival / powered hold samples | What it demonstrates |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| [First g5 handoff](../runs/robot-calibration/wrist-mount-validation-20261120/g5-higher-handoff-recording-01/) | 173 / 173 | 1 / 30 | One mixed startup event, followed by all-powered verification, movement and a recorded hold. Recovery records operator power removal. |
| [Fixed-side g5](../runs/robot-calibration/wrist-mount-validation-20261120/g5-fixed-side-recording-02/) | 381 / 381 | 1 / 30 | Longer stream completed on one connection with camera and encoder evidence. |
| [Empty e4 rehearsal](../runs/robot-calibration/wrist-mount-validation-20261120/e4-empty-matched-recording-02/) | 682 / 682 | 2 / 30 | Two reviewed motion sections completed after one seed. The operator did not have to cycle power between sections. |

**What to carry forward:** identify the owner, verify all six motors and actual gains, seed once, retry only the permitted reads, then stream reviewed goals and record feedback throughout. If a guard trips, its trace determines the next action. The successful runs establish this sequence for supervised placement on this arm. They do not establish a zero failure rate.
